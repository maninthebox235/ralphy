🎯 Quick Start Tips

Before the Visit

  • ✅ Review patient chart and previous DAX notes
  • ✅ Ensure DAX app is open and ready
  • ✅ Check microphone permissions and audio quality
  • ✅ Have template phrases ready for common scenarios

During Recording

  • 🎤 Speak clearly and at normal pace
  • 🎤 Use full medical terms (avoid abbreviations when speaking)
  • 🎤 Structure your conversation chronologically
  • 🎤 Mention key findings as you examine

📋 Essential Command Phrases

Starting the Visit

"This is a [new/follow-up/surveillance] visit for [patient name]"
"Patient is here for [chief complaint]"
"Patient has a history of [relevant oncology diagnosis]"

History Taking

  • "Patient reports..."
  • "She states..."
  • "She denies..."
  • "On review of systems, positive for... negative for..."
  • "Family history includes..."
  • "Social history: patient is a [occupation], [tobacco/alcohol/drug use]"

Physical Exam

  • "On physical examination..."
  • "Vital signs show..."
  • "General appearance is..."
  • "Breast exam reveals..."
  • "Pelvic exam shows..."
  • "Lymph node exam is unremarkable/shows..."
  • "Abdominal exam reveals..."

Assessment & Plan

  • "My assessment is..."
  • "The plan is to..."
  • "I will order..."
  • "I discussed with the patient..."
  • "Patient understands and agrees with the plan"
  • "Follow-up in [timeframe]"

🏥 Women's Health Oncology Templates

Breast Cancer Surveillance Visit

Opening:

"This is a follow-up visit for [patient name], a [age]-year-old female with a history of [stage] [type] breast cancer, diagnosed in [month/year], status post [treatment: lumpectomy/mastectomy, chemotherapy, radiation]. She is currently on [hormonal therapy/none]."

Key Elements to Mention:

  • Time since diagnosis and treatment completion
  • Current medications (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, etc.)
  • Medication adherence and side effects
  • Symptoms: pain, masses, skin changes, nipple discharge
  • Lymphedema assessment
  • Menopausal symptoms if relevant
  • Bone health status

Exam Language:

"Breast exam: Surgical site is well-healed with no masses, skin changes, or lymphedema. Opposite breast shows no masses or abnormalities. Lymph node exam is unremarkable."

Ovarian Cancer Follow-up

Opening:

"This is a surveillance visit for [patient name] with a history of [stage] [type] ovarian cancer, diagnosed in [month/year], status post [surgical debulking/hysterectomy/BSO], chemotherapy regimen of [specific agents], completed [date]."

Key Elements:

  • CA-125 trends (mention if rising/stable/declining)
  • Abdominal/pelvic symptoms: bloating, pain, early satiety
  • Bowel/bladder function
  • Neuropathy or other chemo side effects
  • Quality of life assessment

Exam Language:

"Abdominal exam shows no masses, organomegaly, or ascites. No lymphadenopathy appreciated."

Cervical Cancer/Dysplasia Visit

Opening:

"Patient presents for [follow-up colposcopy/surveillance] for [CIN 2/3, cervical cancer stage X, HPV-positive high-grade lesion]."

Key Elements:

  • Previous biopsy results and dates
  • Treatment history (LEEP, cone biopsy, chemoradiation)
  • HPV status
  • Abnormal bleeding or discharge
  • Pain with intercourse

Exam Language:

"Pelvic exam: External genitalia normal. Speculum exam shows [cervix appears normal/lesion at X o'clock position]. Bimanual exam is non-tender with no masses."

Endometrial Cancer Follow-up

Opening:

"This is a post-treatment visit for [patient name] with [stage/grade] endometrial cancer, status post [hysterectomy/BSO], [adjuvant radiation/chemotherapy], completed [date]."

Key Elements:

  • Vaginal bleeding or discharge
  • Pelvic or back pain
  • Weight changes
  • Hormone replacement therapy discussion if relevant
  • Lymphedema screening

🔑 Optimization Tips

1. Use Medical Terminology Correctly

  • Say the full term: "human papillomavirus" not "HPV" (first mention)
  • Be specific: "Stage 2A estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative invasive ductal carcinoma"
  • DAX recognizes standard medical terms better than shortcuts

2. Structure Your Speech

Follow this order consistently:

  1. Chief Complaint
  2. History of Present Illness
  3. Past Medical History (oncology-specific)
  4. Medications
  5. Review of Systems
  6. Physical Examination
  7. Assessment
  8. Plan

3. Be Explicit About Negatives

  • "She denies chest pain, shortness of breath, and palpitations"
  • "No nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea"
  • "No new lumps or masses"
  • This helps with billing and compliance

4. Mention Time Frames

  • "Symptoms started three weeks ago"
  • "Last chemotherapy was two months ago"
  • "Follow-up in three months"

5. State Lab Values

  • "CA-125 today is 15, which is stable from last visit"
  • "Hemoglobin is 11.2, down from 12.5 last month"
  • "White blood cell count is within normal limits"

6. Document Shared Decision Making

  • "I discussed the risks and benefits of continued surveillance versus intervention"
  • "Patient prefers to proceed with..."
  • "We reviewed the treatment options including..."
  • "Patient's questions were answered to her satisfaction"

7. Prescriptions & Orders

Be specific when mentioning:

  • "I am prescribing [medication name] [dose] [frequency] for [indication]"
  • "I am ordering a [test name] to evaluate [indication]"
  • "I am referring to [specialist] for [reason]"

💡 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ Avoid ✅ Better
"Um, uh, let me see..." Pause recording, then resume
Abbreviations: "s/p, h/o, PMH" "Status post, history of, past medical history"
Side conversations Pause during irrelevant talk
Mumbling exam findings Speak clearly: "Breast exam is unremarkable"
"She's fine" "Patient is doing well, no new concerns"
Forgetting to close "This completes the visit" or "End of encounter"

📊 Billing & Compliance

Level of Service Documentation

To support higher-level billing codes, mention:

  • Complexity: "Given the patient's complex oncology history..."
  • Time: "I spent 45 minutes in face-to-face time with the patient, more than half of which was spent in counseling and coordination of care"
  • Risk: "High complexity medical decision-making given..."

Required Elements Checklist

For each visit, verbally cover:

  • Chief complaint
  • Relevant history
  • Pertinent physical exam
  • Review of systems (at least 2-9 systems for detailed)
  • Assessment with ICD-10 relevant diagnoses
  • Plan with clear next steps
  • Patient education and shared decision-making

🔧 Technical Troubleshooting

If DAX Misunderstands

  • Speak more slowly
  • Enunciate clearly
  • Spell unusual terms: "The diagnosis is L-E-I-O-M-Y-O-M-A"
  • Use "period" or "comma" for punctuation

Audio Quality Issues

  • Position microphone 6-12 inches away
  • Minimize background noise
  • Use exam room with good acoustics
  • Test recording before first patient

Missing Information

  • Review the generated note immediately
  • Add addendums through DAX or EHR
  • Use DAX's edit feature for corrections

🎓 Pro Tips

  1. Create Mental Templates: Have 3-5 templates memorized for your most common visit types
  2. Practice Your Flow: Record a few practice visits to find your rhythm
  3. Review & Refine: Check your first 10 notes daily and adjust your speaking style
  4. Use Keywords: DAX recognizes certain keywords like "assessment," "plan," "physical exam"
  5. Pause Strategically: Use the pause button during interruptions
  6. Review Immediately: Edit while the visit is fresh in your mind
  7. Batch Editing: Set aside 15 minutes after your morning and afternoon sessions
  8. Share with Team: Compare notes with colleagues to learn best practices

🌟 Success Metrics

Track your improvement:

  • ✅ Time saved per note (goal: 5-7 minutes)
  • ✅ Note accuracy (fewer edits needed)
  • ✅ Documentation completeness
  • ✅ Patient satisfaction (more face-time)
  • ✅ Reduced end-of-day charting

📝 Sample Complete Note Script

Scenario: Breast Cancer Surveillance Visit

"This is a six-month surveillance visit for Jane Smith, a 54-year-old female with a history of stage 2A estrogen receptor-positive, progesterone receptor-positive, HER2-negative invasive ductal carcinoma of the left breast, diagnosed in March 2024. She underwent left breast lumpectomy with sentinel lymph node biopsy in April 2024, followed by adjuvant chemotherapy with dose-dense AC-T, completed in September 2024, and adjuvant radiation therapy completed in November 2024. She is currently on anastrozole one milligram daily.

Patient reports she is tolerating anastrozole well with mild joint stiffness in the morning that resolves with activity. She denies any new breast masses, skin changes, nipple discharge, or arm swelling. She reports hot flashes occurring about five times per day which are manageable. No bone pain. She is exercising regularly and maintaining her weight. She denies any fevers, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss.

Past medical history is significant for hypertension and hypothyroidism, both well-controlled. Current medications include anastrozole one milligram daily, levothyroxine 75 micrograms daily, and lisinopril 10 milligrams daily. She has no known drug allergies.

On review of systems, she denies chest pain, shortness of breath, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, changes in bowel or bladder habits. She endorses the hot flashes and joint stiffness as mentioned.

Physical examination: Vital signs show blood pressure 128 over 78, heart rate 72, temperature 98.6 Fahrenheit. General appearance: well-appearing, in no acute distress. Breast exam: Left breast surgical site is well-healed with no masses, skin changes, or erythema. Right breast shows no masses, skin changes, or nipple discharge. No axillary or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy bilaterally. Chest is clear to auscultation. Cardiovascular exam reveals regular rate and rhythm. Abdomen is soft, non-tender, non-distended with no masses or organomegaly.

My assessment is that this is a 54-year-old female with stage 2A hormone receptor-positive breast cancer, status post lumpectomy, chemotherapy, and radiation, currently on anastrozole, doing well with no evidence of recurrence. She is tolerating hormonal therapy with manageable side effects.

The plan is to continue anastrozole one milligram daily. I reviewed the importance of medication adherence. For her joint stiffness, I recommended regular exercise and stretching, and she may take over-the-counter ibuprofen as needed. We discussed that she will need bone density screening, which I am ordering today. I am also ordering bilateral screening mammogram and left breast tomosynthesis to be completed within the next month. She will continue to follow up with me in six months or sooner if she develops any concerning symptoms including new lumps, persistent pain, or significant changes in side effects. We discussed red flag symptoms including severe bone pain, neurological changes, or persistent cough that would warrant earlier evaluation. Patient verbalized understanding of the plan and all her questions were answered. This completes the visit."
