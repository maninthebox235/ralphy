🩺 Ovarian Cancer
▼

Opening - Surveillance Visit

"This is a surveillance visit for [patient name] with a history of [stage] [type] ovarian cancer, diagnosed in [month/year], status post [surgical debulking/hysterectomy/BSO], chemotherapy regimen of [specific agents], completed [date]."

Opening - New Diagnosis

"Patient presents with newly diagnosed [stage] ovarian cancer. She underwent [diagnostic surgery/biopsy] on [date] showing [histology]. She is here to discuss treatment options."

Key Points to Cover

  • CA-125 trends - "CA-125 today is [value], which is [rising/stable/declining] from [prior value] on [date]"
  • Symptoms: abdominal/pelvic pain, bloating, early satiety, change in appetite
  • Bowel/bladder function - constipation, diarrhea, urinary frequency
  • Chemo side effects: neuropathy, fatigue, cytopenias
  • Quality of life and functional status
  • Ascites - new or worsening abdominal distension

Physical Exam

"Abdominal exam shows no masses, organomegaly, or ascites. No supraclavicular or inguinal lymphadenopathy appreciated. Pelvic exam shows no masses or adnexal tenderness."

If Imaging Reviewed

"I reviewed today's CT scan showing [no evidence of disease/stable disease/progression with new findings of...]"

Treatment Plan Examples

"Plan is to continue surveillance with CA-125 and exam every [3 months]. I will order CT imaging in [timeframe] per NCCN guidelines."
🔬 Cervical Cancer/Dysplasia
▼

Opening - Dysplasia Follow-up

"Patient presents for [follow-up colposcopy/surveillance] for [CIN 2/3, HSIL], previously treated with [LEEP/cone biopsy/cryotherapy] on [date]."

Opening - Cancer Surveillance

"Patient with history of [stage] cervical cancer, [histology: squamous cell/adenocarcinoma], diagnosed [month/year], status post [chemoradiation/radical hysterectomy], completed [date]. Here for surveillance visit."

Opening - New Cancer Diagnosis

"Patient presents with newly diagnosed cervical cancer, [stage] [histology], identified on biopsy [date]. Here to discuss treatment planning."

Key Points to Cover

  • HPV status - co-testing results, high-risk subtypes
  • Previous treatments - LEEP, cone biopsy, excisional procedures with dates
  • Pathology - CIN grade, margins, depth of invasion
  • Symptoms: abnormal vaginal bleeding, post-coital bleeding, discharge
  • Pain - pelvic pain, dyspareunia
  • Bowel/bladder symptoms if advanced disease

Physical Exam - Colposcopy

"Colposcopy performed. Cervix visualized after application of acetic acid showing [acetowhite lesion at X o'clock/no acetowhite changes]. Biopsies obtained at [locations] and sent to pathology. Endocervical curettage performed."

Physical Exam - Surveillance

"Pelvic exam: External genitalia normal. Speculum exam shows [cervix appears normal/well-healed post-treatment/lesion at X o'clock position measuring approximately X cm]. Vaginal walls appear normal. Bimanual exam shows no pelvic masses, normal-sized uterus, no parametrial thickening or nodularity."

For Post-Radiation Patients

"Speculum exam shows expected radiation changes to vaginal mucosa and cervix. No evidence of recurrent disease. Vaginal vault is well-healed."

Assessment & Plan Examples

"Plan for continued surveillance with pelvic exam and HPV co-testing in [3-6 months] per guidelines. Patient counseled on symptoms requiring earlier evaluation."
🫀 Endometrial Cancer
▼

Opening - Surveillance

"This is a post-treatment visit for [patient name] with [stage] [grade] [type: endometrioid/serous/clear cell] endometrial cancer, status post [total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy], [adjuvant radiation/chemotherapy/both], completed [date]."

Key Points to Cover

  • Vaginal bleeding or discharge - any post-menopausal bleeding
  • Pelvic or back pain
  • Abdominal symptoms - bloating, ascites
  • Weight changes
  • Lymphedema - lower extremity swelling
  • Menopausal symptoms if premenopausal at diagnosis

Physical Exam

"Speculum exam shows vaginal vault is well-healed with no masses or lesions. No abnormal discharge. Bimanual exam shows no pelvic masses or nodularity. Abdomen is soft, non-tender, no masses or organomegaly. No lower extremity edema."
🎗️ Breast Cancer
▼

Opening

"This is a follow-up visit for [patient name], a [age]-year-old female with a history of [stage] [type] breast cancer, diagnosed in [month/year], status post [treatment: lumpectomy/mastectomy, chemotherapy, radiation]. She is currently on [hormonal therapy/none]."

Key Points

  • Time since diagnosis and treatment
  • Current medications (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors)
  • Side effects and adherence
  • New symptoms: pain, masses, skin changes
  • Lymphedema assessment

Exam

"Breast exam: Surgical site is well-healed with no masses, skin changes, or lymphedema. Opposite breast shows no masses or abnormalities. Lymph node exam is unremarkable."
💬 Essential Phrases
▼

Starting

  • "Patient is here for [chief complaint]"
  • "Patient has a history of [diagnosis]"

History

  • "Patient reports..."
  • "She denies..."
  • "On review of systems, positive for... negative for..."

Physical Exam

  • "On physical examination..."
  • "Vital signs show..."
  • "General appearance is..."

Assessment & Plan

  • "My assessment is..."
  • "The plan is to..."
  • "I will order..."
  • "I discussed with the patient..."
  • "Follow-up in [timeframe]"
💡 Quick Tips
▼
  • Use full terms: "human papillomavirus" not "HPV" (first mention)
  • State negatives: "She denies chest pain, shortness of breath"
  • Include time frames: "Last chemotherapy was two months ago"
  • Mention lab values: "CA-125 today is 15, stable from last visit"
  • Document decisions: "I discussed risks and benefits..."
  • Be specific with orders: "I am prescribing [drug] [dose] [frequency] for [indication]"
